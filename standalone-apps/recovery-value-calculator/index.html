System Reference

Inputs

  • Part identity (category, brand, condition, quantity) — auto-filled from part number where the catalog has a match
  • Resale market data: a comp price and a stated confidence in that comp
  • Scrap data: material, weight, current scrap price
  • Precious metal data: metal type, content per unit, spot price, refiner payout %
  • Parts-out data: gross value of components sold separately, component count, disassembly labor
  • Shared cost data: labor, packaging/shipping, platform/payment fees, return risk, holding cost, time-to-sell

Logic — five paths, computed in parallel

Scrap = (Weight × ScrapPrice) − StripLabor

Resale = (CompPrice × ConditionMultiplier) × (1 − Fees%) − Labor − Ship/Pack − Holding − HazmatFee

Condition multiplier: New/NOS and Tested-working = 100% of comp; Untested = 65%; Parts/damaged = 30%; Salvage only = 0% (Resale excluded).

Parts-out = GrossPartsValue × (1 − Fees%) − DisassemblyLabor − (Ship/Pack × ComponentCount) − Holding

Precious Metal = (Content × SpotPrice × RefinerPayout%) − ExtractionLabor

Discard = −DisposalCost (0 unless the part requires paid disposal, e.g. hazmat/e-waste fees)

Breakeven Comp = [(NetScrap + Labor + Ship/Pack + Holding + HazmatFee) ÷ (1 − Fees%)] ÷ ConditionMultiplier

Resale is excluded from comparison when comp confidence is None or condition is Salvage only. Parts-out is excluded when gross parts value is $0 (nothing entered). Precious Metal is excluded unless the checkbox is enabled. Scrap and Discard are always computed — Discard is the honest "just throw it away" baseline every other path has to beat.

The recommended path is whichever computed value is highest. If the leader beats the runner-up by under 15%, the tool flags it as a close call rather than a clean win. When Discard wins, that means every recovery path — including scrapping it — nets zero or worse, so keeping the part costs you shelf space and future handling time for no payoff.

Outputs

  • Recommendation stamp: SCRAP / RESALE / PARTS-OUT / PRECIOUS METAL / DISCARD
  • All five net values side by side (n/a shown for excluded paths)
  • Breakeven comp price — the minimum sold price (pre-condition-adjustment) that still beats scrapping
  • Lot-level total across quantity on hand, net of market-depth routing when Resale wins

Failure Modes

  • No comp data — Resale is excluded rather than guessed. Get a comp before overriding.
  • Misidentified part or counterfeit/relabeled hardware — comp price attaches to the wrong item; verify part number against brand documentation before trusting the comp.
  • Undisclosed damage on "tested" units — inflates return risk beyond the 3% default and can invalidate the condition multiplier; raise return risk or downgrade condition for anything not bench-verified.
  • Scrap price volatility — metal prices move independently of eBay comps; treat the scrap number as a floor, not a fixed price.
  • Precious metal content is a guess until measured or assayed — content-per-unit and spot price are placeholders you supply; treat Precious Metal output as provisional until a refiner or assay confirms actual content.
  • Parts-out gross value is entirely manual — the tool has no data on what individual components are worth; an unverified number here will overstate Parts-out.
  • Hazmat/battery items — some carriers restrict lithium battery shipping; confirm shippability before committing to Resale or Parts-out.
  • Brand discontinuation risk — a discontinued lock line can spike (scarcity) or crash (dead platform) in value; treat comps on discontinued lines as lower-confidence by default.

Governance

  • Log every evaluation (Decision Log tab) with the inputs used, not just the outcome — enables later accuracy review across all four paths.
  • Recalibrate scrap and precious-metal spot prices monthly; recalibrate eBay comps whenever a category goes 60+ days without a fresh sold listing.
  • Review closed listings and actual refiner/parts-buyer payouts quarterly against the logged prediction to check whether the 15% margin threshold, condition multipliers, and refiner payout % still hold.
  • Any RESALE decision made with Comp Confidence = Low, or any PRECIOUS METAL decision with unmeasured content, should be treated as provisional and revisited once real data comes back.

Data Freshness — no live feed

This artifact runs sandboxed in the browser with no outbound network access, so it cannot call a pricing API or refresh itself. Scrap and precious-metal defaults shown here were pulled by manual web search and hard-coded as of Aug 30, 2026: steel/brass/aluminum from dealer scrap boards (ScrapMonster, scrapmetalbuyers.com, Sgt. Scrap NJ); gold/silver/palladium/copper spot from Kitco, JM Bullion, and TradingEconomics COMEX quotes. Every one of these moves daily — gold alone swung roughly $150 in the week these were pulled.

To refresh: ask for updated prices in chat and the numbers here get re-pulled and re-loaded — this is a manual refresh on request, not a standing feed. Treat the values as a same-week reference point, not a quote you can act on directly; confirm against your scrap yard's board and a live spot chart before finalizing any payout.

Watch-outs

  • Comp price and comp confidence are still 100% manual — there is no eBay/marketplace comp feed behind this tool, live or otherwise.
  • Scrap grades vary widely by prep and purity (e.g., red brass ~$4.15/lb vs. mixed brass turnings ~$2.80/lb) — the single "brass" default is a clean-grade midpoint, not your specific lot's price.
  • The 15% margin threshold, condition multipliers (100/65/30/0%), and default fee/labor/payout rates are starting assumptions; they should be tuned to actual BenchTested outcomes, not treated as fixed.
  • Category-level weight/material/labor defaults applied by the part-number lookup are averages, not per-part data — verify before trusting Scrap or Precious Metal on high-value or high-volume parts.
  • Log data is stored locally to your account in this artifact and is not shared or exported automatically — export or transcribe periodically if you need it elsewhere.
